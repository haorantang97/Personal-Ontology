# Archive stage

Use this reference only in the distillation-and-knowledge-archive task.

## Outcome

Continue from the last confirmed archive checkpoint, preserve the interview's detail in Raw/evidence layers, and promote only durable, useful, properly bounded knowledge into the result layer. The archive task is the second stage of the same life-review skill.

## Inputs and continuity

Accept one or more of:

- completed or interim Raw;
- structured handoff;
- related materials referenced by the interview;
- a precise transcript range when artifacts have not yet been produced.

First determine:

1. the last successfully archived or handed-off event;
2. the earliest subsequent interview content not yet transferred;
3. whether any apparent gap is merely “discussed in chat” rather than actually present in an artifact or knowledge proposal;
4. whether the new material overlaps an existing package.

Never claim continuity merely because the events were previously discussed. Verify the handoff/artifact/proposal boundary.

## Light cleaning before distillation

Keep the detail. Only:

- remove confirmed background audio and mechanical duplicate fragments;
- normalize punctuation and obvious recognition mistakes;
- apply user-confirmed corrections;
- label uncertain terms, dates, numbers, and sequence;
- group adjacent passages by event without rewriting the user's meaning.

Do not collapse months of experience into a short timeline, erase emotional texture, or convert colloquial meaning into polished but weaker abstractions.

## Evidence-aware distillation

Separate every meaningful claim into one principal category:

1. explicit first-person factual claim;
2. feeling or self-explanation;
3. external evidence;
4. AI candidate hypothesis;
5. uncertainty or contradiction.

Check roles and causation. Preserve negative evidence and later revision. Stable patterns about ability, values, work style, or repeated failure require at least two independent events, a competing explanation or counterexample, contextual limits, and user confirmation.

## Knowledge-base execution

For any formal knowledge change:

1. Call the knowledge-base intake contract before proposing changes.
2. Search reusable result knowledge first; inspect evidence scope only when provenance or deduplication requires it.
3. Prefer a small update to an existing page over creating a duplicate.
4. Route detailed Raw and source evidence separately from reusable result knowledge.
5. Produce an exact proposal that shows what will be added, updated, excluded, and why.
6. Do not write until the user explicitly approves that exact proposal.
7. After writing, verify the write and indexing result and report the new archive checkpoint.

The archive task may ask a compact set of blocking factual questions, but it must not drift into another open-ended interview. Send substantial new narration back to the interview stage and produce a new handoff.

## Result-layer boundaries

- Store only knowledge that can change future judgment or action and has a clear evidence/use boundary.
- Keep Raw transcripts, chat flow, and unclean handoffs out of default retrieval results.
- Do not create a generalized personality dossier.
- Do not broaden private material's use through anonymization or summarization.
- Do not publish third-party accusations or group generalizations as facts.
- If the evidence supports only “the user understood it this way,” preserve exactly that boundary.

## Archive completion receipt

Report:

- the exact interview range covered;
- the Raw/handoff materials consumed;
- pages proposed or changed;
- material intentionally left only in Raw/evidence or excluded;
- unresolved evidence or contradictions;
- the last archived checkpoint and the next interview node.
