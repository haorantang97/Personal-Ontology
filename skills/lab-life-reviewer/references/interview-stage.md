# Interview stage

Use this reference only in the interview-and-material-collection task.

## Outcome

Recover a detailed, correctable account of one bounded life event while keeping momentum across the larger life timeline. The interview should feel conversational, not like filling out a form, but it must still reach a usable stopping point and create durable artifacts.

## Choose the interaction mode

### Live voice mode

- Keep replies short enough not to interrupt the user's recall.
- Ask one focused question at a time. Avoid stacked alternatives and long prefatory interpretations.
- Treat `结束` as “the current answer is finished” unless the user clearly says the whole topic or session should end.
- Acknowledge only the essential correction or newly learned point, then continue. Do not repeatedly recite the whole timeline.
- Do not force the user to choose from emotions or meanings proposed by the interviewer. Let the user name the experience in their own terms.
- When the transcript contains background audio, duplicated fragments, or obvious recognition errors and the user identifies them as such, exclude them from the event record.
- If inspecting a file would materially answer the next question, briefly tell the user and inspect it; otherwise preserve conversational flow and defer heavier analysis to a natural pause.

### Text mode

- Responses may be somewhat more structured, but keep the exchange conversational.
- Inspect linked materials before asking for content already present in them.
- Ask one principal question at a time; a short batch is acceptable only for closely related factual gaps that are easier to answer together.
- Show concise interim summaries when they help the user correct the record, not after every answer.

## Interview movement

Use a flexible trust and information curve rather than a rigid questionnaire:

1. Start from a concrete scene, event, artifact, or decision.
2. Establish chronology, people, role, and observable actions.
3. Follow the mechanism: how it began, what changed, what was difficult, and how decisions were made.
4. Explore costs, failures, tradeoffs, and attempted corrections after enough context exists.
5. Ask what the user now thinks the event meant, while preserving that as self-interpretation rather than objective causation.
6. Close the event and move to the next chronological or user-selected node.

Prefer questions that recover mechanisms and examples:

- “当时最先发生的具体事情是什么？”
- “这一步里你本人实际做了什么？”
- “你是什么时候发现原来的方向不对的？”
- “材料里能看到结果，但看不到你当时为什么这么选；这个你怎么理解？”
- “我把你的意思理解成 X，这准确吗？”

Do not force generic startup, career, design, or therapeutic frameworks onto the user's account. A life review follows the event's own logic.

## Use related materials

“当前主题相关材料” is intentionally broad. It can include PDFs, portfolios, images, videos, recordings, files, posts, contracts, scores, messages, financial records, project artifacts, or earlier interview notes.

When material exists:

1. Establish what it is and which event it belongs to.
2. Inspect its structure and directly observable content.
3. Use it to resolve chronology, names, outputs, and visible details.
4. Separate what the material shows from what the user remembers or interprets.
5. Gather remaining questions and ask only those that matter.

Do not assume every material is reliable, complete, contemporaneous, or authorized for public use.

## Prevent endless interviewing

Maintain a silent coverage check for the current event:

- trigger/background;
- chronology and setting;
- user's role and concrete actions;
- important people and role boundaries;
- decisions and alternatives;
- difficulties, costs, failure, or conflict;
- observable result;
- aftermath and later correction;
- present-day interpretation;
- evidence, uncertainty, and privacy;
- next life event.

This is not a form to read aloud. Once the important items are covered, say that the event has enough detail, identify any non-blocking uncertainties, create the handoff, and move on. Do not ask for exact dates or figures when they will not change future use.

## Preserve the record

- Keep Raw close to the user's wording and order. Remove only obvious duplicated transcription, confirmed background audio, and purely technical noise; mark gaps instead of repairing them by inference.
- Record later corrections alongside the earlier version. Use the corrected version for current chronology while retaining the correction trail.
- Lightly normalize names and punctuation only when confidence is high; otherwise mark the term for verification.
- Never reduce a long event to only a few timeline bullets. The handoff must retain representative examples, process detail, emotional meaning in the user's own framing, and unresolved contradictions.

## Closing the interview stage

Close a topic when its main arc is usable, not when every possible question is exhausted. Confirm only privacy or interpretation boundaries that are genuinely unclear. Then produce the Raw and handoff defined in [handoff-schema.md](handoff-schema.md), state the next event, and mark the package ready for the archive task.
